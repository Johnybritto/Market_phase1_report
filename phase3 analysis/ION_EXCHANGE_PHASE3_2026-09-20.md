## Executive Conclusion

Ion Exchange remains one of the **highest-quality strategic water franchises** in the listed Indian universe because it combines:

- Water-treatment systems and engineered solutions.
- Ion-exchange resins.
- Membranes.
- Specialty water-treatment chemicals.
- Industrial and ultra/high-purity water.
- Wastewater reuse and ZLD.
- Lifecycle services, O&M, consumables, spares, BOO/BOOT/WaaS and digital monitoring.
- Consumer water products.

That makes Ion Exchange a very different business from a plain municipal EPC contractor and, strategically, one of the best possible complements to WABAG.

However, **the current financial evidence does not justify buying the stock merely because the franchise is attractive**.

FY26 consolidated revenue grew only about 6.5%, while EBITDA/PAT weakened materially. FY26 PAT fell roughly 31%, consolidated operating cash flow turned negative, borrowings increased sharply, capital expenditure remained heavy and ROCE fell. Q1 FY27 then produced a severe profit collapse despite 20% revenue growth: EBITDA margin fell to roughly 4.5% and PAT fell to about ₹3 crore.

The core issue is therefore no longer whether Ion Exchange participates in the water theme. It clearly does.

The questions are:

1. Can **Treatment Solutions** stop destroying consolidated profitability?
2. Can the new **Roha resin facility** move from startup cost drag to utilisation-led operating leverage?
3. Can the company convert reported earnings back into **operating cash flow**?
4. Can debt and working capital stabilise while capacity ramps?
5. Is today's premium valuation already discounting much of the expected recovery?

### Current Phase 3 action

> **AWAIT FUNDAMENTAL TRIGGER / AVOID FRESH BUY AT CURRENT EVIDENCE LEVEL.**

This is **not** a rejection of Ion Exchange as a business. It is a decision to demand operating proof before deploying fresh capital.

### Preferred recovery gates before upgrading

At least two of the following should become visible:

- Treatment Solutions EBIT turns positive or losses reduce materially for two consecutive quarters.
- Specialty Chemicals EBIT margin recovers toward **12–15%+**, showing Roha startup drag is easing.
- FY27 consolidated operating cash flow turns clearly positive.
- Net debt / borrowings stop rising materially.
- Consumer Products reaches break-even without sacrificing growth.
- Roha utilisation progresses toward management's targeted first-year ramp.

### Single thesis-disproof metric

> **FY27 consolidated operating cash flow remains negative.**

If a full year of Roha ramp-up, higher revenue and improving mix still fails to generate positive operating cash flow, the premium-quality franchise thesis needs to be downgraded regardless of the strength of the long-term water narrative.

## 1. Business Overview

Ion Exchange is an integrated water and environmental-management company with more than six decades of operating history.

Its offering spans the full water cycle:

- Raw-water and process-water treatment.
- High-purity and ultra-pure water.
- Demineralisation.
- Industrial wastewater treatment.
- Effluent treatment.
- Zero-liquid-discharge systems.
- Water recycling and reuse.
- Municipal drinking water and sewage treatment.
- Ion-exchange resins.
- Membranes.
- Specialty chemicals.
- Standard water-treatment systems.
- O&M and lifecycle services.
- BOO/BOOT/WaaS models.
- Consumer water products.

The company operates across India and international markets and has more than 200,000 installations and over 3,000 core-industry projects according to its Q1 FY27 investor materials.

### Why this business is strategically attractive

Ion Exchange is not dependent on one revenue engine.

It participates in:

1. **Large engineered projects** — higher ticket, lower recurring quality and more working-capital risk.
2. **Industrial products** — systems, membranes and standardised equipment.
3. **Specialty chemicals/resins** — proprietary products with repeat demand and export potential.
4. **Lifecycle services** — O&M, consumables, spares, rehabilitation, BOO/BOOT/WaaS and digital services.
5. **Consumer products** — smaller but potentially recurring retail/institutional water demand.

This mix is the main reason Ion Exchange deserves a higher-quality classification than another generic water EPC contractor.

That may be reasonable for a high-quality, proprietary water franchise **if**:

- cash conversion improves,
- ROCE recovers,

It is expensive if those assumptions fail.

### Valuation conclusion

At ~₹443:

**Recovery is being anticipated before it is financially proven.**

## 26. Worst-Case Scenario

The realistic bear case is not that Ion Exchange loses its water franchise.

The bear case is:

- Roha utilisation remains slow.
- Specialty Chemicals margins stay around low double-digits or worse.
- Treatment Solutions continues generating losses.
- Consumer Products remains marginal.
- Debt stays elevated.
- CFO remains weak/negative.
- ROCE remains near low-teens.
- The market stops valuing Ion Exchange as a high-growth quality franchise.

In such a scenario, even if revenue keeps growing, the market could rerate the stock from a premium multiple toward a materially lower earnings multiple.

That creates significant downside from an apparently "good business" even without a collapse in revenue.

### What would make this bear case increasingly likely

- Negative FY27 CFO.
- Treatment Solutions negative EBIT through H2 FY27.
- Specialty Chemicals EBIT margin fails to recover above ~12%.
- Borrowings rise further despite capacity commissioning.
- Roha utilisation misses management's ramp expectations materially.
