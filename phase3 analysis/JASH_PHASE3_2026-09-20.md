## Executive Conclusion

JASH is a **high-quality, differentiated water-equipment and export business**, not another generic water EPC company.

It adds a distinct exposure beside WABAG and Ion Exchange:

- **WABAG** — treatment, desalination, reuse, EPC and O&M.
- **Ion Exchange** — resins, membranes, chemicals, consumables, ZLD/reuse and industrial/high-purity water.
- **JASH** — manufactured water-control equipment, gates, screens, valves, process equipment and exports.

Current conclusion:

> **WATCH FOR DIP — HIGH-PRIORITY WATER CANDIDATE. Do not chase at elevated valuation. Preferred accumulation zone: ₹450–475, subject to earnings/cash-flow confirmation.**

The biggest positive is business-model differentiation and export-led order visibility.

The biggest risk is **working-capital deterioration and the gap between strong standalone India performance and weaker consolidated profitability because of overseas/subsidiary drag**.

The single most important thesis-disproof metric is:

> **FY27 operating cash flow / PAT below 60%.**

If profit grows but cash conversion deteriorates materially, the premium-quality manufacturing thesis should be downgraded.

## 1. Business Overview

JASH manufactures engineered equipment used in:

- Water and seawater intake systems.
- Wastewater treatment plants.
- Pumping stations.
- Desalination systems.
- Stormwater infrastructure.
- Water-transmission systems.
- Process industries including power, steel, cement, petrochemicals and chemicals.

The company carries out design, casting, fabrication, machining, assembly and testing internally.

Important operating businesses include:

- Jash Engineering India.
- Rodney Hunt in the United States.
- Waterfront Fluid Controls in the UK.
- Mahr Maschinenbau.
- Jash Process Equipment.

This is materially different from a pure EPC model because JASH manufactures specialised water-control and process equipment rather than simply constructing a complete project.

## 7. Segment / Subsidiary Performance

### India standalone business

Q1 FY27 standalone performance was substantially stronger than consolidated performance.

Approximate Q1 FY27 standalone:

- Revenue: ~₹98.3 crore.
- PAT: ~₹13.4 crore.

This suggests the Indian core business is performing well.

### Consolidated business

Consolidated PAT was only ~₹5.1 crore.

This implies overseas/subsidiary operations absorbed a substantial part of India profitability.

### Jash Process Equipment

Management has highlighted operating/costing issues at Jash Process Equipment.

The business reportedly lost meaningful potential orders while transitioning its manufacturing/costing approach.

This remains an important execution-risk area.

### Rodney Hunt

Rodney Hunt remains strategically important because it gives JASH:

- US manufacturing presence.
- US municipal-water exposure.
- Local customer relationships.
- Better positioning for large US water-control orders.

Management has indicated meaningful FY27 revenue expectations from Rodney Hunt.

However, legacy pre-tariff orders remain a margin risk.

### Key conclusion

Subsidiary-margin normalisation is one of the most important rerating triggers.

## 13. Cash Flow, Receivables and Working-Capital Quality

This is the key caution.

Approximate FY26:

- Operating cash flow: ~₹56 crore.
- PAT: ~₹75.5 crore.
- CFO/PAT: ~74%.
- Free cash flow: ~₹19 crore.

On headline numbers alone, cash conversion appears acceptable.

But underneath:

| Metric | FY25 | FY26 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Revenue | ~₹735 cr | ~₹736 cr |
| Trade receivables | ~₹225 cr | ~₹301 cr |
| Debtor days | ~112 | ~149 |
| Working-capital days | ~71 | ~110 |
| Cash-conversion cycle | ~229 | ~251 |
| ROCE | ~25% | ~18% |

### Critical warning

Receivables rose roughly one-third while revenue was virtually flat.

This is the biggest financial-quality warning in the current JASH thesis.

If working capital continues deteriorating in FY27, JASH should not receive the same premium valuation as a clean cash-generating manufacturing business.

## 24. Worst-Case Scenario

A realistic bear case is not that water demand disappears.

The bear case is:

- JASH continues winning orders.
- US tariffs hurt margins.
- Overseas subsidiaries remain weak.
- Working capital continues rising.
- Overseas capex increases.
- Cash conversion deteriorates.

Under that scenario:

- Revenue may still grow.
- PAT may undershoot management guidance.
- ROCE may remain below historical levels.
- Working-capital days may stay above 100.
- The market may compress the P/E multiple.

The share price could therefore correct materially even while the long-term water theme remains intact.

But the current valuation provides limited protection against a guidance miss.

Visible catalysts:

- Subsidiary recovery.
- Q2/Q3 margin improvement.
- FY27 PAT trajectory.

Conclusion:

Wait for a better risk/reward setup instead of trying to predict an exact market top or bottom.
