# Master Investment Rules — Phase 1 / Phase 2 / Phase 3

**Last updated:** 16 August 2026  
**Repository:** `Johnybritto/Market_phase1_report`  
**Status:** AUTHORITATIVE MASTER RULES

This document is the top-level decision framework for the portfolio, watchlist, Phase 1 scanner and Phase 3 deep dives.

`PHASE_1_2_3_RULES.md` remains the detailed implementation specification for report format, scoring, price-source hierarchy, Phase 1 action taxonomy and the full Phase 3 checklist. If a portfolio or capital-allocation decision conflicts with that file, this master document takes precedence.

The current confirmed portfolio must be read from `PORTFOLIO_HOLDINGS.md`. Holdings and average purchase prices are data, not permanent rules, and must be updated separately whenever positions change.

---

## 1. Primary Objective

Build a high-quality portfolio with a milestone value of **₹10 lakh by 31 March 2027**, while preserving the ability to compound well beyond that target.

### Non-negotiable interpretation

- ₹10 lakh is a **portfolio milestone**, not a forced-investment instruction.
- Reaching ₹10 lakh does **not** mean adding only to stocks already held.
- Reaching ₹10 lakh does **not** justify buying an overvalued stock, averaging a weak business, or deploying cash because a calendar deadline is approaching.
- Existing holdings must compete with new opportunities for every incremental rupee.
- If no opportunity offers acceptable risk/reward, **cash is a valid position**.
- Portfolio quality has priority over hitting the ₹10 lakh date mechanically.

---

## 2. Decision Hierarchy — What Matters Most

Every investment decision must be evaluated in this order:

1. **Business quality** — economics, competitive advantage, management, balance sheet.
2. **Structural growth runway** — whether growth can continue for 3–10 years rather than one or two quarters.
3. **Earnings quality** — revenue growth must translate into EBITDA, PAT and ultimately cash flow.
4. **Valuation** — compare price paid with growth, historical valuation, peers and realistic future earnings.
5. **Portfolio fit** — existing theme exposure, concentration, correlation and position size.
6. **Technical setup / entry timing** — support, trend, retest, breakout quality and downside levels.

### Fundamental vs technical rule

**Fundamentals decide WHAT deserves ownership. Valuation and technicals help decide WHEN to buy it.**

A good chart cannot rescue a weak business. A good business can still be a poor investment when bought at an irrational valuation.

---

## 3. Existing Holdings Have No Automatic Priority

Never allocate fresh money simply because a stock is already in the portfolio.

For every new tranche of capital, ask:

> Is adding to an existing holding the best available risk/reward opportunity in the market today?

If a new company has superior business quality, earnings visibility, valuation and entry setup, the new company gets the capital.

### Forbidden portfolio anchoring

Do not:

- Average every existing holding.
- Buy because the current price is below the user's previous purchase price.
- Avoid buying a quality stock solely because the current price is far above the original purchase price.
- Treat an existing holding as safer merely because it is familiar.
- Force money into the current portfolio to meet the ₹10 lakh target.

Average purchase price is relevant for portfolio P/L, tax and sizing decisions, but **must not become the valuation anchor**.

---

## 4. Fresh Opportunity Search Must Continue

Phase 1 is not only a monitoring engine for current holdings. It must continuously search for superior opportunities across the wider watchlist and market.

Priority discovery patterns include:

- New capacity commissioning or utilisation ramp.
- Operating leverage beginning to appear.
- Margin inflection.
- Order-book acceleration with executable margins.
- Debt reduction / deleveraging.
- Improving cash conversion.
- Export expansion or new geography.
- Import substitution / localisation.
- Industry consolidation.
- Regulatory or policy tailwind.
- Private/public capex cycle beneficiary.
- New product or higher-value mix.
- Second-order AI/data-centre beneficiaries.
- Underfollowed industries with improving economics.
- Structural change that is not yet fully reflected in valuation.

### Fresh-name discipline

Continue looking for **3–5 genuinely qualifying fresh names per week** where possible. Never add a company simply for variety.

A new candidate must be better than at least one realistic alternative use of capital in the existing portfolio.

## 5. Structural India-Growth Themes

The portfolio should remain open to India's multi-year structural growth stories, including but not limited to:

- Defence and aerospace.
- Infrastructure and industrial capex.
- Power generation, transmission and distribution.
- Electrification and cables.
- Electronics manufacturing / localisation.
- Data centres, cooling and electrical infrastructure.
- AI/cloud/cybersecurity infrastructure.
- Financialisation, exchanges and market infrastructure.
- Water and environmental infrastructure.
- Healthcare and pharmaceuticals.
- Manufacturing and export substitution.
- Logistics where industry economics are structurally improving.
- Other hidden or emerging themes discovered through ongoing research.

### Theme rule

A strong theme is **not** permission to ignore valuation, cash flow or business economics.

For each thematic investment, distinguish:

1. Proven current revenue/profit exposure.
2. Future management plan.
3. Optionality / narrative.
4. What is already priced in.
5. The single metric most likely to disprove the thesis.

Avoid unnecessary duplication. A new stock in a theme already represented in the portfolio must add a genuinely different exposure or offer materially better risk/reward.

## 6. Earnings Quality Rules

Revenue growth alone is insufficient.

For every serious candidate, track the chain:

**Revenue → EBITDA → PAT → Operating Cash Flow**

The analysis must explicitly check:

- Revenue growth.
- EBITDA growth and margin direction.
- PAT/EPS growth.
- Operating cash flow versus PAT.
- Free cash flow where meaningful.
- Receivables growth versus revenue.
- Debtor days / working capital.
- Inventory build.
- Debt and interest burden.
- ROCE and ROE trend.
- Capital intensity and capex requirements.
- Promoter pledge and dilution.
- Governance / related-party / regulatory risks.

### Warning pattern

Treat the following as a serious warning rather than celebrating headline revenue growth:

- Revenue +40%, but PAT +10%.
- Revenue growth accompanied by major margin compression.
- Receivables or inventory growing much faster than sales.
- PAT rising while operating cash flow remains weak for extended periods.
- Growth funded mainly through escalating debt/dilution without improving returns.

---

## 7. Valuation Discipline

Never classify a stock as attractive merely because it has fallen from its high.

Never classify a stock as uninvestable merely because it has risen substantially.

Valuation must be judged using a combination of:

- Forward and trailing earnings where reliable.
- Historical valuation range.
- Peer valuation.
- Earnings-growth expectations.
- ROCE/ROE and cash-generation quality.
- Balance-sheet strength.
- Stage of the industry cycle.
- Degree to which the theme is already priced in.

### High-growth rule

High growth can justify a premium valuation, but **the growth must ultimately reach earnings and cash flow**.

### Price-fall rule

A 20–40% correction is not automatically a buy. First determine whether the decline reflects:

- temporary sentiment,
- valuation normalisation,
- earnings disappointment,
- thesis deterioration,
- governance/regulatory risk,
- or a structural business problem.

---

## 8. Buy Rules

A stock should normally receive fresh capital only when most of the following align:

- Business quality is acceptable or strong.
- Structural runway is visible.
- Latest earnings do not contradict the thesis.
- Balance sheet and cash conversion are acceptable.
- Valuation offers a reasonable expected return.
- Portfolio exposure is not already excessive.
- Entry price provides adequate margin of safety.
- Technical structure does not indicate obvious chase risk.

### Staggering

Prefer staged accumulation instead of attempting to identify one perfect bottom.

A Phase 3 report should maintain the three existing buy-range views:

1. Fundamental buy range.
2. Deep technical buy range.
3. Event-risk-adjusted buy range.

The report must state which range currently deserves priority.

---

## 9. Sell / Trim Rules

**Do not sell merely because a stock has doubled, tripled or risen 500%+.**

Large unrealised profit by itself is not a sell signal. Several of the portfolio's best historical outcomes came from allowing strong businesses to compound.

A sell or trim should require one or more of the following:

1. **Thesis break** — the original reason for owning the business is no longer valid.
2. **Earnings deterioration** — growth, margins or return ratios weaken structurally rather than temporarily.
3. **Cash-flow deterioration** — profits are no longer supported by cash generation.
4. **Balance-sheet deterioration** — debt, dilution or working-capital stress becomes excessive.
5. **Governance / promoter / regulatory risk** materially changes the risk profile.
6. **Valuation becomes unjustifiable** even under realistic bullish earnings assumptions.
7. **Concentration becomes excessive** and creates unacceptable portfolio-level risk.
8. **A clearly superior opportunity exists**, after considering taxes, transaction costs and the strength of the existing thesis.

### Re-entry fallacy

Do not sell a high-quality compounder simply because the hope is to buy it back lower later. Re-entry is uncertain and can permanently lose exposure to a long-duration winner.

---

## 10. Portfolio Construction and Concentration

Preferred end-state:

**Approximately 10–15 deeply researched companies + NIFTY BEES/core index exposure + optional cash.**

Do not expand to 20–30 names simply for diversification.

### Concentration review triggers

- A single stock reaching **~15%** of portfolio value should trigger a concentration review.
- A position above **~20%** requires explicit justification and heightened monitoring.
- These are **review thresholds, not automatic sell thresholds**.
- A high-quality winner may temporarily exceed them if the thesis and valuation remain strong.

When the portfolio is concentrated, new capital should preferentially build underweight high-quality opportunities rather than automatically trimming winners.

### Theme concentration

Measure exposure by economic theme, not only by stock count. Two different companies can still represent the same underlying risk factor.

---

## 11. Cash Is an Active Allocation

Cash is permitted when:

- desired stocks are above reasonable buy zones,
- market risk is elevated,
- earnings confirmation is pending,
- valuation offers inadequate expected return,
- or the discovery pipeline has not produced a qualifying idea.

Do not buy mediocre assets merely to remain 100% invested.

Cash should be available to exploit broad corrections or stock-specific dislocations when fundamentals remain intact.

There is no compulsory fixed cash percentage; it should reflect the opportunity set.

## 12. Opportunity Buckets

Every serious stock under consideration should fit one of these decision buckets:

### A. Buy / Accumulate Now
Fundamentals, valuation, portfolio fit and entry setup are all acceptable.

### B. Buy on Correction
High-quality business, but current expected return/margin of safety is insufficient. Define a realistic buy zone.

### C. Await Fundamental Trigger
The theme/business is interesting, but evidence is incomplete. Examples: margin recovery, capacity utilisation, cash-flow improvement, debt reduction, new order execution or higher-value revenue mix.

### D. Discovery / Phase 3 Candidate
Promising idea that requires deeper validation before capital is committed.

### E. Reject / Avoid Fresh Buy
Business quality, governance, valuation, balance sheet, liquidity or risk/reward does not justify capital.

These portfolio buckets coexist with the fixed Phase 1 action taxonomy in `PHASE_1_2_3_RULES.md`; the Phase 1 report format itself must not be changed unless explicitly requested.

## 13. Long-Term (5+ Year) Classification

A stock should be called a **5+ year core holding** only when there is evidence for:

- Durable competitive position.
- Multi-year addressable market runway.
- Credible management/capital allocation.
- Healthy balance sheet.
- Acceptable long-term cash conversion.
- Sustainable or improving ROCE.
- Ability to survive normal industry cycles.
- Valuation that does not require impossible future assumptions.

A theme alone does not qualify a stock as a permanent core holding.

Every 5+ year holding must still be reviewed when the thesis or economics materially change. "Long term" must never mean "never reassess."

## 14. Phase 1 Integration

Phase 1 must continue using the existing detailed report rules, but the scanner should additionally respect these principles internally:

- Existing holdings receive no automatic scoring advantage.
- Current portfolio concentration must affect capital recommendations.
- Fresh candidates should be surfaced when their risk/reward exceeds that of existing holdings.
- A stock can be a good long-term business but still receive `Watch for Dip` or `Avoid Fresh Buy` at an excessive valuation.
- A fallen stock should not receive `Accumulate` unless fundamentals and valuation justify it.
- Current holding status should inform concentration/risk warnings, not bias the fundamental conclusion.
- The full watchlist and hidden-theme search should continue even when several existing holdings are performing well.

---

## 15. Phase 3 Integration — Mandatory Capital-Competition Test

Every full Phase 3 for a possible purchase must answer:

1. Is the business fundamentally good enough to own?
2. Is the growth thesis proven or mostly narrative?
3. Is cash flow supporting reported earnings?
4. Is current valuation justified by realistic growth?
5. What price creates an adequate margin of safety?
6. What would disprove the thesis?
7. How does this exposure overlap with current holdings?
8. **Is this a better use of the next rupee than adding to the best existing alternative?**
9. What is the correct action: Buy / Accumulate / Wait / Hold / Trim / Avoid?

A Phase 3 should be decisive. Do not default to diplomatic language when evidence supports a strong conclusion.

---

## 16. Evidence and Freshness Rules

Investment decisions must use current, verifiable information.

Priority order:

1. NSE/BSE/company filings and investor relations material.
2. Earnings releases, presentations and conference-call commentary.
3. Regulatory filings / shareholding / exchange disclosures.
4. Reliable market data sources for price and valuation cross-checks.
5. Reputable financial news for context.
6. Brokerage commentary only as secondary evidence, not the primary thesis.

If price, corporate-action adjustment, order-book data or a key fundamental metric cannot be validated, explicitly mark it as uncertain rather than inventing precision.

## 18. Final Decision Standard

For each important portfolio decision, the final answer should be clear enough to act on.

Prefer conclusions such as:

- **Core 5+ year hold — do not sell for normal volatility.**
- **Good business, bad current price — wait.**
- **Add only below X / after Y fundamental trigger.**
- **Do not average — earnings/cash flow do not justify it.**
- **Trim because concentration/valuation has become excessive.**
- **Exit because the thesis has broken.**
- **New candidate is superior to adding to existing holdings.**

Do not soften a negative conclusion simply because the stock is already owned.

Do not become bearish solely because a stock has generated a large profit.

Do not become bullish solely because a stock belongs to an attractive India-growth theme.

---

## 19. Core Principle

> **We are not trying merely to reach ₹10 lakh. We are building a ₹10 lakh portfolio that deserves the opportunity to become ₹25 lakh, ₹50 lakh and beyond.**

Every new rupee must compete for its place.
